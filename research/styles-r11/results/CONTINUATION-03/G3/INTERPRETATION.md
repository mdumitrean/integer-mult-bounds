## Whether joint contractions have a distinct premise

Individual profile neutrality does not imply joint neutrality in a general
frame problem. Two edits with disjoint literal register supports can still
couple through later shared gate vertices in the global frame-constraint graph.
Removing two constraints may permit a choice that removing either alone does
not. A proof of additive independent costs requires a separator or disjoint
affected factor-graph components, with their boundary labels fixed. Disjoint
triangles alone are insufficient evidence for that stronger statement.

Thus a separately registered finite test of the120 unordered pairs of these16
contractions and their full16-member batch would ask a different question:
whether collective frame optimization has a nonzero mixed cost difference.
The single-contraction null supplies the exact marginal costs for that test;
it is not erased or retrospectively enlarged. A joint test must construct each
combined word from original source IDs, prove its scalar identities and
nonoverlap guards, rebuild one fresh frame graph, charge the whole profile,
and retain the same native/global admission boundary. No joint test was run
while writing this document. The common-frame locations and exact neutrality
give little direct evidence for favorable interaction, so this is a bounded
falsifier, not a predicted improvement.
